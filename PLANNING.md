# Molecular Pathology — A Primer for the Pathology Rotation

## Planning package: objectives first, then the build

> Built for **Macallan & Hussein** — medical students rotating in pathology.
> Educator: academic pathologist. Companion to the Transfusion Medicine one-day crash course.

**Design constraint that governs everything:** the primer is taught in **one sitting of 1–2 hours** (planned at about 90 minutes). Anything that does not fit that sitting lives in clearly separated *supplementary* material that students can open on their own. The page must never feel overwhelming.

---

## 1. How the objectives were derived

The objectives were mapped, top-down, from three sources rather than invented:

| Source | What it contributed |
|---|---|
| **ABPath Molecular Genetic Pathology Content Specifications** — the framework ABPath publishes for molecular content on the AP/CP examination, with each item designated **C** (Core/Foundational), **AR** (Advanced Resident) or **F** (Fellow) | The spine. The guided session covers the **C** items (the central dogma, oncogenes and tumor suppressors, cytogenetics, PCR, colorectal mismatch repair and others) together with the **AR** fundamentals a rotating student meets every day on service — specimen handling, real-time PCR, sequencing, FISH, arrays and variant classification — taught at an introductory depth. Remaining **AR** items are flagged "Next level". **F** items appear only as "Preview" mentions in the supplementary material. |
| **ABPath Clinical Pathology Content Specifications (April 2026)** | Where molecular testing sits inside hematology (cytogenetic testing, clonality, translocations/mutations), microbiology (targeted molecular assays, sequence-based identification, molecular resistance testing), chemistry (pharmacogenetics) and transfusion medicine. |
| **CP-in-5 blueprint work** — the Molecular Pathology chapter map (37 episodes) with its distilled concept layer, plus the board-review molecular and cytogenetics episode maps | The high-yield facts that recur on boards and on service: pre-analytic traps (heparin, acid decalcification, formalin), probe designs, Ct logic, MMR/MSI, HER2 reflex testing, the classic translocations, repeat expansions, pharmacogenes. |

**Learner assumption:** a clinical student who knows what cancer, infection and inheritance are, has heard of PCR and "sequencing", and has never had to choose a molecular test, protect a specimen for one, or read a molecular report.

**Teaching rule that follows:** teach the *decisions* (which test, on what specimen, meaning what) before the catalogue of facts.

## 2. The eight learning objectives (one per stop)

By the end of the session, the learner can:

| # | Objective | ABPath MGP section(s) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read the language.** Trace a gene from DNA to protein, name the six kinds of alteration, and read a variant name such as *BRAF* c.1799T>A, p.Val600Glu. | §1 Normal structure and function; §2 Molecular genetic principles | 12 min |
| 2 | **Protect the specimen.** Choose the tube and fixative, avoid the acid-decalcification trap, and estimate tumor cellularity. | §4 Pre-analytical considerations | 8 min |
| 3 | **Amplify.** Explain PCR, reverse-transcription PCR and real-time PCR, and read a Ct value. | §3 Techniques (PCR, RT-PCR, NAAT, qPCR); §5 Controls | 10 min |
| 4 | **Sequence.** Compare Sanger with next-generation sequencing, and use depth, variant allele fraction and limit of detection correctly. | §3 Techniques (sequencing, NGS) | 10 min |
| 5 | **See chromosomes and choose the test.** Match karyotype, FISH, microarray, PCR and sequencing to the size and kind of lesion each can detect. | §3 Techniques (cytogenetics, FISH, arrays) | 10 min |
| 6 | **Interpret the report.** Separate germline from somatic findings, use the tier languages, handle a variant of uncertain significance, and state what a negative result does not exclude. | §4 Variant classification, reporting; §16 Genomic databases | 10 min |
| 7 | **Molecular in cancer.** Link the core results to an action: HER2, EGFR, KRAS, BRAF, mismatch repair, HPV, BCR::ABL1, PML::RARA, JAK2. | §10 Solid tumors; §11 Hematopathology | 10 min |
| 8 | **Beyond cancer.** Recognise when molecular testing answers an inherited-disease, drug-response or infection question, and the consent and privacy rules that travel with it. | §7 Indications; §8–9 Inherited disease and cancer syndromes; §12 Pharmacogenetics; §13 Infectious disease; §6 Ethical and legal | 7 min |

Orientation (5 min) and wrap-up with two cases and the take-home list (8 min) bring the plan to **90 minutes**. Skipping the stops' "if time allows" activities compresses it to about 60; discussing every case stretches it to 120.

### Level flags used throughout

The flags are teaching levels for this audience. They are guided by the ABPath designations rather than copied from them one to one: ABPath marks many everyday techniques (FISH, real-time PCR, sequencing, pre-analytic handling, variant classification) as "AR", and a rotating student still needs their basics.

- **Core** — what a rotating student should own: the ABPath "C" items, plus the introductory level of those everyday "AR" fundamentals.
- **Next level** — seen on service and worth recognising: most ABPath "AR" items.
- **Preview** — ABPath "F". Named so it is not a surprise; supplementary only.

In the biomarker atlas, where each row is a single ABPath item, the flag follows the ABPath designation of that item directly.

## 4. Information architecture

```
Session (≈ 90 min)
  Start here → 1 Language → 2 Specimen → 3 Amplify → 4 Sequence
  → 5 Chromosomes → 6 Interpret → 7 Cancer → 8 Beyond cancer → Wrap-up
On your own (supplementary)
  Case lab · Question bank · Biomarker atlas · Reference tables · Glossary
```

Every stop follows one rhythm: a short framing paragraph → one interactive → a "Carry this away" list of three or four points → one checkpoint question → a collapsed "Go deeper" drawer. A session clock in the header shows elapsed time against the plan.

## 6. Assessment strategy

- **Checkpoints** — one question inside each stop, mapped to that stop's objective.
- **Question bank** (supplementary) — a mixed bank filterable by objective; every question carries the answer, the reasoning, why the distractors fail, and a teaching pearl.
- **Cases** — two worked in the wrap-up; six more in the supplementary case lab.
- **Objective map** — each objective shows correct/total for its own questions; progress is stored per learner on the device.
